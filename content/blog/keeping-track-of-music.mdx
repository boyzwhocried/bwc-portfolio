most music apps are very good at giving you more. more new releases, more recommendations, more playlists that disappear into the feed before you have decided whether they matter.

i did not need another stream of music. i needed a way to stay with the music i had already found.

## discovery is not the same as keeping

there is a difference between hearing a song, liking it, and knowing where it belongs. the first two happen quickly. the last part takes a little attention.

so the collection has a small holding area. new songs can sit there without being forced into a permanent home. some get a second listen. some fit a mood. some only make sense next to a different group of songs. some never return.

that delay is useful. it stops every first impression from becoming a decision.

## playlists as places

the best playlists are not shelves for genres. they are places with a point of view.

one might hold songs for a particular hour. another might collect a texture that is hard to name. another can be a record of a month, not because the month was important, but because those songs were there when it happened.

the names matter less than the feeling that each place has a reason to exist. when a song does not fit, it is fine to leave it in motion for a while.

## structure without the scoreboard

there is a temptation to quantify taste until it stops feeling like taste. top artists, listening minutes, and neat summaries can be fun, but they are not the point.

the point is return. a song becomes part of a life when it survives the first rush, shows up again, and still has something to say.

the system is only there to make that return easier. it keeps the pile from becoming noise, while leaving enough room for accidents. that is the whole job.
