for about two years i worked insurance data through an IT consultancy: they placed me, they invoiced the client, i showed up and did the work. it's a normal arrangement in this market. it's also a little precarious, because your actual employer is not the place you spend your days.

this month the paper changed. i signed a new six-month contract with a different outsourcing company, and i'm still in the same seat at the same client. it is not a direct hire, and i'm not going to dress it up as one.

## what didn't change

the work is the same shape: SSIS pipelines, staging layers, a data warehouse that has opinions about how insurance products should be modeled. same domain knowledge, same systems, mostly the same people around me day to day.

## what did change

the vendor, the contract terms, and the term itself. six months is short, and that is on purpose: it keeps my options open instead of locking me in. the offer also made me sit down and look at what the work is worth instead of accepting the first figure.

## the part nobody warns you about

the timesheet system changed. the onboarding paperwork changed. the small procedural stuff ate more of the first week than the actual technical handover did. if you've ever switched vendors under the same client, you already know: the code doesn't change, the bureaucracy does.

still insurance data. still suspicious of every column that claims to be clean.
