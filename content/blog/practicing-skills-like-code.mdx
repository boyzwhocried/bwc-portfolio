for a long time my plan for getting better at anything soft (negotiating, pricing my own work, holding a hard conversation) was "try to remember to do it better next time." that is not a plan. it is a hope wearing a plan's clothes.

## the actual failure mode

it was never a knowledge problem. i could describe what good negotiation looks like. i could explain the pricing psychology behind a number. none of that transferred to the moment where i actually had to say the number out loud to someone.

knowing the theory and doing the rep are two different systems, and only one of them improves the skill.

## what changed it

two things, and neither of them is clever:

**a fixed slot.** the skill gets a scheduled, recurring point in the week where it happens, not a "whenever i feel ready" window that mostly stays empty. willpower loses to a bad day. a calendar slot doesn't ask how you're feeling.

**a person.** a rep that only i witness is a rep i can quietly skip or fudge. a rep someone else is expecting is a rep that has to actually happen. this is the entire mechanism, and it is almost insultingly simple: convert "improve at X" into "show up to this slot" or "do this in front of this person," and the abstract goal turns into a concrete, checkable action.

## what this looks like now

a running catalog of the specific skills i'm actually working (not a vague "communication" bucket, actual named gaps), each with the concrete first rep that gets it out of theory. the catalog is knowledge. it does nothing by itself. the only part that moves the number is standing in the room and doing the uncomfortable thing on schedule.

the system's job was never to make the rep easier. it was to make skipping the rep harder than doing it.
